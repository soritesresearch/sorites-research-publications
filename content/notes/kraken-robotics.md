## Executive conclusion

Kraken Robotics is a legitimate, technically differentiated subsea-sensing company, not merely a defense-theme promotion. Its core synthetic-aperture sonar is unusually compact, power-efficient, vehicle-agnostic, and capable of centimetric resolution at commercially useful survey rates. The Covelya acquisition adds respected navigation, communications, optical, software, and autonomy products, transforming Kraken from a specialist sonar-and-battery supplier into a broader subsea instrumentation platform.

But three parts of the popular thesis currently outrun the evidence:

1. Kraken is not demonstrably *the* global subsea sensor winner. Kongsberg and Thales offer established integrated systems and operational pedigrees that a compact-payload comparison does not capture.
2. Kraken has not disclosed ownership of customer-generated seabed or defense data, nor a business selling pooled historical datasets. Its present "data business" is principally customer-specific survey deliverables and licensed processing/visualization software.
3. The recent share-price decline is not purely a cosmetic de-rating. Valuation compression explains much of it, but Covelya's first-half 2026 results show a real slowdown, and Kraken still must prove cash conversion, integration and second-half guidance.

At C$4.00, the valuation is no longer built on the near-perfect execution implied by the former high. It still assumes a successful integration and substantial EBITDA growth. If combined EBITDA eventually reaches C$140–150 million, the present valuation may prove conservative; if integration or cash conversion falters, it leaves meaningful room for further compression.

That dilution bought a substantial profitable business using shares issued at C$8.50. The economic outcome depends on the earnings and cash flow acquired. It becomes destructive if Covelya's decline proves structural or if integration absorbs the expected synergies.

## 4. Technical assessment

### Synthetic-aperture sonar

Synthetic-aperture sonar combines consecutive acoustic pings into an effective long array. Unlike conventional side-scan sonar, its theoretical along-track resolution does not deteriorate directly with range. The trade-offs are motion-estimation requirements, processing complexity and sensitivity to trajectory errors and difficult seabed conditions. [NOAA technical overview](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/technology/sonar-sas/) and the [IEEE Journal of Oceanic Engineering review](https://doi.org/10.1109/joe.2009.2020853).

Kraken’s published SAS specifications include:

- real-time imagery around 3.3 × 3.0 cm;
- post-processed imagery around 2.1 × 1.9 cm;
- a 337 kHz centre frequency;
- 300-, 1,000- and 6,000-metre depth configurations, depending on model;
- range up to 200 metres per side, depending on configuration and speed;
- a SAS-120 example of approximately 1.88 km²/hour at four knots and 181 metres per side;
- integration into new and existing underwater vehicles.

These are vendor specifications rather than independent comparative field tests. They support the case for a compact, high-resolution payload, but do not establish superiority across all operating conditions. [Kraken SAS fact sheet](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/SAS_Kraken_Flyer_Letter.pdf).

KATFISH combines an actively stabilized towbody with SAS imaging. Kraken’s current product page advertises standard operations at 4–10 knots, range up to 200 metres per side, area coverage up to 3.5 km²/hour, 3 × 3 cm real-time imagery and 2 × 2 cm post-processed imagery. These figures are configuration-dependent vendor specifications. [KATFISH product page](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/products/katfish/).

### Competitive position

Kongsberg's HUGIN/HISAS 1032 advertises approximately 1,000 metres of swath at 2.5 knots and typical 5 × 5 cm imagery. It offers much more coverage, but on a larger, complete AUV platform and at a very different system cost and power envelope. [Kongsberg HUGIN/HISAS description](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/stories/2018/12/next-generation-hugin-here/).

Thales' SAMDIS family provides operationally deployed multi-view SAS and is embedded in established mine-countermeasure programs. Public comparable specifications are sparse, which prevents a defensible direct ranking. [Thales PathMaster and SAMDIS](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/solutions-catalogue/defence/naval/pathmaster-unmanned-mine-countermeasures-solutions).

Sonardyne's Solstice, now inside Kraken, is a multiple-aperture rather than synthetic-aperture sonar. It offers a 200-metre swath at only approximately 18 watts, making it complementary for smaller vehicles where power and volume matter more than maximum SAS resolution. Sonardyne itself describes MAS as a lower-size-and-power alternative while acknowledging SAS's resolution and range advantages. [Solstice specifications](https://www.sonardyne.com/product/solstice/) and [MAS-versus-SAS explanation](https://www.sonardyne.com/mas-vs-sas-when-should-we-use-multiple-aperture-sonar/).

**Finding:** Kraken plausibly has one of the best compact, platform-independent SAS payloads. The public record does not establish universal superiority over Kongsberg or Thales across coverage, classification performance, adverse environments and complete-system reliability. NATO research also cautions that high-frequency sonar performance deteriorates in complex, partially buried and cluttered target environments. [NATO CMRE 2023 report](https://www.cmre.nato.int/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/CMRE-AR-2023.pdf).

### SeaPower batteries

SeaPower uses pressure-tolerant, silicone-filled NMC lithium-ion packs. Kraken advertises:

- up to 260 Wh/L and 145 Wh/kg;
- operation to 6,000 metres;
- greater than 2,000 discharge cycles at zero bar to 80% retained capacity;
- configurations from 1.8 kWh to multi-megawatt-hour systems;
- UN 38.3 qualification;
- a customer endurance increase from approximately 40 to more than 100 hours.

[SeaPower product page](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/products/seapower/) and [2025 technical book](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Kraken-Tech-Book-SeaPower-2025compressed.pdf).

The defensibility assessed here lies in pressure-tolerant packaging, battery-management systems, safety qualification and vehicle integration. Those capabilities create switching costs, but the battery business remains exposed to cell supply, safety failures, pricing pressure and vehicle manufacturers internalizing pack design.

The installed content can nevertheless be large: the technical book describes XL-AUV systems using up to 64 batteries, with large standard modules around 18–23 kWh. That makes SeaPower an important economic component rather than a peripheral accessory.

### Navigation, communications, optics and autonomy

Covelya changes the technical thesis materially:

- Sonardyne SPRINT-Nav combines INS, DVL and depth sensing, with published navigation performance as low as 0.01% of distance travelled in certain configurations. [SPRINT-Nav](https://www.sonardyne.com/product/sprint-nav/)
- BlueComm provides short-range, high-bandwidth underwater optical communication, supplementing low-bandwidth acoustic links. [BlueComm 100](https://www.sonardyne.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Sonardyne_8360_BlueComm_100.pdf)
- Voyis provides underwater laser scanning and optical imaging.
- Wavefront provides forward-looking obstacle-avoidance sonar.
- EIVA's NaviSuite connects acquisition, navigation, processing, visualization and supervised ROV autonomy.

EIVA's ROV Autonomy can perform waypoint, stand-off and velocity-control tasks and is vehicle-agnostic through a published interface. It is best described as supervised task automation, not independent general autonomy; the operator remains responsible for mission oversight. [NaviSuite ROV Autonomy](https://www.eiva.com/products/software/navisuite/navisuite-bundles/navisuite-rov-autonomy).

The combined architecture is strategically compelling:

**power → navigation → acoustic/optical communications → sonar/optical sensing → processing → operator interface/autonomy**

Kraken can now cross-sell across that chain and remain independent of any single AUV or ROV manufacturer. That vehicle-agnostic position is probably more valuable than owning a complete vehicle whose success depends on one platform design.

## 5. Installed-base economics

The combined group describes more than 700 customers and substantial deployment across defense, survey, offshore energy and research markets. Qualification cycles and vehicle integration can produce durable supplier relationships because changing sonar, navigation or power systems requires mechanical, electrical, software and safety revalidation.

Potential recurring sources include:

- replacement and expanded battery capacity;
- sonar upgrades and processing modules;
- maintenance, calibration and field support;
- rental equipment;
- survey services;
- EIVA software subscriptions and support;
- additional sensors placed on an already-qualified vehicle family.

EIVA offers annual or daily subscriptions, perpetual licences and support/maintenance services. [NaviSuite licensing](https://www.eiva.com/products/navisuite-software).

But the disclosed economics are still primarily hardware-oriented. Covelya's 2025 revenue was approximately 81% products, 4% software and 8.5% rental. Kraken has not disclosed installed-unit counts, software ARR, renewal rates, attach rates, customer cohorts or service gross margins.

The installed-base flywheel is therefore a credible hypothesis, not yet a quantified recurring-revenue engine. The disclosed revenue mix does not support a vertical-SaaS comparison.

## 6. Seabed data and licensing rights

Kraken's published service terms say each party retains its pre-existing intellectual property and that new intellectual property created by Kraken while performing services belongs to Kraken. They also impose confidentiality obligations. They do **not** expressly establish that Kraken owns raw customer survey data or may pool, resell, license or use that data to train models. Individual proposals, government contracts and classified-program terms can override the general document. [Kraken service terms](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/KRS-GRP-COM-POL-002-Kraken-Robotics-General-Terms-and-Conditions-of-Services.pdf).

A naval survey contract, for example, describes processing and delivering high-resolution seabed products to navy representatives, not Kraken retaining commercial rights to those products. [Survey-contract example](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/news-releases/kraken-awarded-3-million-seabed-survey-contract/).

EIVA’s September 2026 terms reserve intellectual-property rights in its software and in items developed while performing services. Those provisions are broader than software ownership alone, but they do not, by themselves, establish blanket rights to pool and commercially relicense customer survey data. This is an analytical reading of the public standard terms; individual agreements and applicable law determine actual rights. [EIVA licence terms](https://www.eiva.com/Files/EIVA-Files/PDF/Terms/TERMS%20AND%20CONDITIONS%20-%20LICENSE%2010.09.26.pdf).

Actual disclosed data-related products include:

- high-resolution sonar imagery and bathymetry;
- processed seabed-survey deliverables;
- optical 3D models and digital twins;
- inspection and environmental datasets prepared for customers;
- NaviSuite processing and visualization licences;
- cloud hosting/viewing of customer project data.

The materials reviewed for this note do not identify a revenue line for licensing pooled historical seabed data, a third-party dataset marketplace, or customer permission to reuse classified or operational data commercially.

**Analytical treatment:** proprietary data licensing carries no value in the scenarios below. A future consensual data network may be valuable, particularly using non-sensitive commercial survey and inspection data, but it would require explicit contractual rights, customer consent, security segregation and a disclosed commercial product. The intellectual leap from "good sensors" to "owns the world's underwater data" is unsupported by the present evidence; Kraken already has a credible instruments, software and services business without an invented data moat.

## 7. Is the decline merely a de-rating?

### Evidence for a valuation reset

Applying the reconstructed current all-securities count to the former C$10.72 high implies more than C$4.1bn of equity value. This is a constant-share-count valuation comparison, not the actual market capitalization on the date of the high. Against approximately C$88m of combined 2025 adjusted EBITDA (the company’s earlier pro-forma estimate, combining Kraken’s audited results with estimated Covelya results in its [May 2026 investor presentation](https://www.krakenrobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kraken-May-Investor-Presentation-Final.pdf)), that represented roughly **47× equity value to that earlier EBITDA estimate**, before allowing for net debt. Such a valuation required near-flawless growth, integration and defense enthusiasm.

At C$4.00 and approximately C$1.53bn EV:

- EV/combined 2025 adjusted EBITDA: approximately **17.4×**;
- EV/reported 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance: approximately **20–24×**.

These are different denominators. The C$88m measure is the earlier combined 2025 estimate, while reported 2026 guidance includes only six months of Covelya. Neither is an audited full-year 2026 combined run rate.

Much of the decline is therefore rational multiple compression from an exuberant peak.

### Evidence of a genuine adverse change

There are also real negatives:

- Covelya first-half revenue fell 5%, and operating profit fell 39%.
- Kraken's standalone first-half EBITDA margin was 16%, below the full-year combined target.
- A customer reduced project scope by C$1.5m.
- Kraken recorded a C$6.9m arbitration provision.
- Both businesses experienced weak first-half cash conversion.
- Inventory and capital requirements are rising.
- Share count expanded by more than 40%.
- Purchase accounting, integration costs and the actual closing balance sheet remain unknown.
- The guidance requires a substantially stronger second half.
- Publicized orders are not the same as funded, executable backlog.

**Conclusion:** the decline is a mixture of de-rating and legitimate execution concern. There is no public evidence supporting a claim that institutions deliberately forced the price lower to accumulate shares. That may be a market narrative, but it is not a research conclusion.

### Bear case

Covelya's slowdown persists, Kraken misses guidance, orders convert slowly, working capital consumes cash, and integration costs offset synergies. A conventional industrial-defense multiple produces substantial downside.

### Base case

Covelya recovers, Kraken reaches approximately C$120m of combined EBITDA, C$10m of synergies are substantially realized and the balance sheet remains near neutral. Substantial realization during 2027 is this scenario’s assumption; management’s 24-month target runs to July 2028.

### Bull case

Defense UUV procurement, offshore infrastructure monitoring and subsea robotics accelerate simultaneously. Kraken achieves C$150m of EBITDA, demonstrates recurring software/service attachment and earns a premium sensor-platform multiple.

A return toward the prior C$10.72 high would require approximately C$4.1bn of diluted equity value. Assuming neutral net debt, even an 18× enterprise-value/EBITDA multiple would require approximately C$230m of sustainable EBITDA. That comparison uses the reconstructed current all-securities count; it does not recreate historical capitalization. The old high should therefore not be treated as an automatic recovery target.

## 9. What would change the picture

The picture improves if the next two consolidated reports establish:

1. Covelya's first-half decline was timing-related and revenue returns to double-digit growth.
2. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA reaches a repeatable C$30m-plus quarterly run rate.
3. Operating cash flow tracks EBITDA after normal working-capital fluctuations.
4. Management publishes a clean backlog measure with order-to-revenue conversion.
5. Software, rental, support and replacement revenue grow faster than hardware.
6. Cross-selling appears in named contracts across sonar, navigation, power and software.
7. Net debt remains modest after purchase accounting and final adjustments.
8. No further material arbitration, warranty or integration provisions emerge.

A disclosed commercial-data strategy, with clear customer ownership provisions, would be incremental upside, but it is not necessary for the core analysis.

The picture worsens if the reports show continued contraction at Covelya, repeated reliance on adjusted earnings without cash conversion, further working-capital strain, rising net debt, project cancellations, margin deterioration, additional material provisions, or another equity issuance before the acquired business demonstrates its expected earnings contribution.
