**SORITES RESEARCH** · Locate the Grain

# The \$300 Million Question

*Why aren’t AI giants buying the physical world? GoPro’s customer relationships may be the asset, and capturing them may not require buying the company.*

Drew Clement · Prepared November 12, 2026 · Public edition 1.0

Strategic research · Physical AI · Distressed acquisitions · No securities recommendation

> **THE CALL IN 60 SECONDS**
>
> **The puzzle.** AI companies are spending billions on machines that learn from the physical world. A company that already captures that world, and already licenses customer footage for AI training, is being sold under a merger agreement that offers \$285 million of aggregate cash, subject to adjustment, and leaves existing holders about 10% of the surviving public company.
>
> **Our view.** GoPro’s existing customer relationships may lower the cost of building a task-specific capture network. An acquisition premium depends on proving an advantage that a contract cannot deliver.
>
> **What we do not claim.** That buying GoPro beats licensing its data, commissioning new collection or hiring its team. The evidence does not yet say that.
>
> **How we can be wrong.** Section 10 separates public developments we will monitor from economic tests that need diligence, and says what missing evidence does and does not mean.

## **1. A company that records the world is running out of runway**

The AI industry can spend heavily on machines that learn from the world while a company that helps record it struggles to finance another quarter.

GoPro makes that tension concrete. It has cameras, imaging engineers, software, subscribers and a working business licensing customer video for AI training. It also has creditors, operating losses and a signed merger agreement. A strategic buyer would acquire all of those together unless it could negotiate a narrower transaction.

**Our thesis is that GoPro’s existing customer relationships may lower the cost and time of building a task-specific capture network.** That is the commercial question. The evidence does not yet establish that buying the company beats licensing its data, partnering for recruitment, commissioning new collection or hiring a team. An acquisition premium depends on proving an advantage that a contract cannot deliver.

That distinction decides whether this is an acquisition opportunity or an appealing story attached to a troubled stock.

## **2. Why scale changes the search**

Capital changes which assets deserve a look. NVIDIA reported \$56.6 billion in cash, equivalents and marketable debt securities at July 26 <sup>\[2\]</sup>. Meta reported \$82.45 billion in cash, equivalents and marketable securities at September 30, alongside negative quarterly free cash flow <sup>\[3\]</sup>. Neither company’s market capitalization is an acquisition budget.

Our illustrative \$300 million common-share purchase requires \$650 million of total funding. That is about 1.15% of the NVIDIA liquidity figure. It could be financeable and still be a poor acquisition, because management attention, integration cost and alternative uses of capital are scarce.

The question was sparked by a public debate about how much capital a single AI leader could raise <sup>\[1\]</sup>. That debate is speculation and we do not rely on it.

## **3. Four assets an AI buyer could want**

Four assets inside GoPro could matter to an AI buyer. They depend on the operating business, so whether they can be separated is something to test, not a given. Each has a narrower route and each needs a different test.

|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Audience**           | 2.18M subscribers in Q2 2026 and 50M cumulative cameras sold <sup>\[7, 8\]</sup>. Neither counts active contributors. Subscribers declined 11% year-over-year; retention of annual subscribers was 67% in Q2, versus 68% a year earlier <sup>\[8\]</sup>. Standard eligibility requires adults resident in the U.S. or its territories with a Premium or Premium+ subscription; employees have a residence exception <sup>\[9\]</sup>.                        | Active, connected, consenting cohorts, upload frequency, and the cost to recruit for a specific task. | Recruitment or co-marketing partnership                                                                 |
| **Library**            | More than 450 petabytes stored and 500,000+ opted-in hours. Stored volume is not rights-cleared content <sup>\[4, 5\]</sup>.                                                                                                                       | Rights chain, consent records, task relevance and what has already been sold.                         | License. Exclusive control of the content would require additional contributor rights; GoPro’s existing license is nonexclusive |
| **Software and cloud** | A subscription platform and a telemetry format that can preserve synchronized sensor data <sup>\[5, 10\]</sup>.                                                                                                                                       | Whether delivery workflows keep the fields robots need, and whether the engineers stay.               | Software partnership or license; hire or retain engineers                                                             |
| **Design and IP**      | The 10-K/A reports 1,608 issued U.S. and 868 corresponding foreign patents at Dec 31, 2025 <sup>\[5\]</sup>. ### **The recruiting idea**

The most interesting asset is the first one. Anyone who already owns a GoPro already owns capture hardware, and GoPro already has a billing and rewards relationship with a subset of them. If a program turned those owners into paid contributors for specific tasks, a buyer could build a task-specific capture network faster than by recruiting from scratch.

**Separate two kinds of saving.** Existing cameras save kit spending, but in our illustrative \$35 million build case hardware is \$5 million, about 14%. The larger opportunity has to come from cheaper recruitment, faster delivery, higher acceptance rates or repeat collection. None of those is proven, and GoPro has not published the numbers.

**What the consent terms do and do not do.** The program terms grant GoPro a perpetual, nonexclusive, sublicensable and transferable license for AI-model training, and GoPro may license to the AI developers it selects <sup>\[9\]</sup>. The terms apply to content participants upload to GoPro’s cloud and make available to the program. A rights audit must establish whether existing permissions cover the proposed content and downstream use; commissioned tasks may require additional permissions or agreements, depending on their scope. Owners of rival cameras can be recruited by anyone, so the advantage is the relationship and the tooling, not the hardware.

> Compare fully loaded cost and delivery time per accepted episode with the best alternative. GoPro has not published those economics; neither have we.

## **4. GoPro is already selling an AI input**

**What GoPro reports.** The voluntary AI Training Program was announced July 30, 2025 <sup>\[4\]</sup>. By the first week of March 2026 the company reported more than 500,000 opted-in hours, and its second-quarter release reported \$2 million of AI-content licensing revenue <sup>\[5, 6\]</sup>. The program’s reach and stored volume are in the asset table above.

**What is missing.** Repeat purchasing, delivered and accepted hours, contribution margins and relevance to robotics. Multiplying one quarter by four gives arithmetic, not a forecast.

### **A buyer would inherit a license, not the footage**

Participants keep title to their content. GoPro holds the license described above and may freely assign the agreement, so a buyer of GoPro would inherit that framework. Rewards are expected to approximate half of license fees after related costs and expenses. Opting out limits new use cases, while prior deliveries can retain rights <sup>\[9\]</sup>. Consent records and customer contracts decide what a buyer can actually use.

> A transferable license can be commercially valuable. A nonexclusive license can also be available without owning the licensor.

## **5. The scarce input is a useful episode**

A robot learning to fasten a component needs more than a view of someone’s work. It may need camera calibration, timing, hand or gripper motion, contact information, task boundaries and a reliable record of success or failure. A camera’s inertial sensor measures its own movement; it does not supply every action a robot must execute.

GoPro’s telemetry format can preserve synchronized sensor information in original files <sup>\[10\]</sup>. Whether a given cloud-delivery workflow keeps the fields a buyer needs must be tested on samples.

There is a real robotics link. The original Universal Manipulation Interface used GoPro cameras on handheld grippers to record demonstrations <sup>\[11\]</sup>. That supports investigating GoPro’s engineering. It also shows researchers can buy its cameras without buying the manufacturer.

The substitution risk is already visible. NVIDIA’s current GEAR UMI documentation describes replacing GoPro with an OAK-4P top camera and a USB trigger camera, alongside additional sensing <sup>\[12\]</sup>. NVIDIA’s November GR00T 2 research reports pretraining on roughly 402,730 hours of egocentric human video, 10,184 hours of handheld demonstrations and 23,500 hours of robot teleoperation <sup>\[13\]</sup>. Buyers already run collection programs, so GoPro must add information they do not have.

> **The economic unit is a rights-cleared episode that improves a defined model or evaluation.** Petabytes and historical camera sales are poor substitutes for that measurement.

## **6. The deal clock**

GoPro is not waiting to be discovered. It hired an adviser and ran a process. Its October preliminary proxy describes competing proposals and a signed transaction with Starman Optical <sup>\[14\]</sup>. That raises the burden of proof for any claim that the market overlooked these assets. It does not identify any unnamed process participant as an AI company.

| **Date**     | **Event**                                                                                                                 |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| May 2026     | GoPro opens a strategic review                                                                                            |
| Sept 1, 2026 | Starman merger announced: \$285M aggregate cash and about 10% continuing ownership for existing holders <sup>\[15\]</sup> |
| Oct 2026     | Preliminary proxy describes competing proposals and the sale process <sup>\[14\]</sup>                                    |
| Nov 10, 2026 | Late-filing notice: Q3 10-Q delayed, covenant waivers sought, going-concern doubt maintained <sup>\[17\]</sup>            |
| Nov 6, 2026  | Amendment extends the merger outside date from Dec 31, 2026 to Mar 31, 2027 <sup>\[24, 25\]</sup>                      |
| Nov 12, 2026 | GoPro files the amendment; it requires reasonable best efforts to extend the financing commitment but does not establish that an extension was obtained <sup>\[24, 25\]</sup> |
| Jan 5, 2027  | Date by which the 2021 Credit Agreement balance must be repaid under Amendment No. 4 <sup>\[5\]</sup>                     |
| Feb 10, 2027 | Sorites scorecard date (Section 10)                                                                                       |

The counterparty is Starman Optical, and the proxy describes the transaction entities as formed for the merger <sup>\[14\]</sup>. The Parent entity is Action Acquisition LLC; the November amendment corrected an earlier plural rendering of its name <sup>\[25\]</sup>. The executed agreement converts each eligible old share into \$1.14 of conditional cash plus 0.1 surviving share. Cash falls with a contractual working-capital shortfall. The agreement contains a no-shop, a qualified superior-proposal route, matching rights and a \$10 million termination fee under specified circumstances <sup>\[16\]</sup>. A competing buyer needs an executable contractual path, not a higher headline number.

The November 10 notice establishes risk, not an outcome. Without waivers, defaults and cross-defaults could allow acceleration, which could jeopardize the merger <sup>\[17\]</sup>. It is not an actual acceleration or a terminated deal.

The November 12 filing extends the contractual outside date to March 31, 2027. It does not extend the January 5 lender repayment date disclosed in the 10-K/A, and it does not establish that the merger financing commitment was extended. The amendment obliges the Parent to use reasonable best efforts to obtain that extension <sup>\[24, 25\]</sup>. More calendar time is not the same as more liquidity.

Distress can lower an asset’s price while damaging the people, supplier relationships and continuity that made it interesting. Waiting for a cheaper restructuring is not costless.

## **7. Three prices, three funding commitments**

The cases assume a cash purchase of all common shares, as independent hypothetical alternatives to Starman. Each includes an assumed \$50 million for warrants and awards, \$100 million of face debt and \$20 million of usable excess cash. Operating leases stay in operating costs. Closing balances and security settlements are unverified.

| **USD millions**                                       | **\$300M case** | **\$500M case** | **\$1B case** |
|--------------------------------------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Common-share consideration                             | 300             | 500             | 1,000         |
| Equity consideration incl. assumed warrants and awards | 350             | 550             | 1,050         |
| Illustrative enterprise value                          | 430             | 630             | 1,130         |
| Total funding through 18 months                        | 650             | 850             | 1,350         |

The \$220 million between enterprise value and total funding is made of assumed debt premiums and accrued interest (\$20M), transaction fees (\$25M), a contingent Starman fee (\$10M), integration and retention (\$40M), working-capital funding (\$25M) and operating cash losses (\$100M). These assumptions are visible on purpose. None is a management forecast. Adding every balance-sheet liability to the bridge would double-count debt, and an accounting warrant liability cannot be added again after its contractual settlement is included.

### **The price has to earn a return**

With no terminal value and no credit for the legacy business, a stylized ten-year stream beginning after an 18-month ramp needs about **\$136 million** of annual unlevered free cash flow to cover the \$650 million envelope at a 12% discount rate. The \$850 million and \$1.35 billion cases need about **\$178 million** and **\$283 million**. This is a reverse-underwriting test, not a forecast and not a claim that data licensing can produce those sums.

## **8. Why these buyers might pass**

The relevant buyer is the one whose roadmap benefits enough from control to pay for it. Financial capacity alone does not rank the candidates.

| **Potential buyer or partner**       | **Possible strategic benefit**                                             | **Strongest reason to prefer a narrower arrangement**                                |
|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **NVIDIA**                           | Instrumented capture, imaging expertise, a broader robotics data ecosystem | Existing collection infrastructure, substitute sensors, partner neutrality           |
| **Alphabet / DeepMind**              | More demonstrations for embodied models and difficult real environments    | Established robotics partners and simulation capabilities                            |
| **Meta**                             | Wearable imaging, stabilization and complementary capture formats          | Its own glasses platform and overlapping hardware                                    |
| **OpenAI**                           | Capture engineering and multimodal inputs for a device roadmap             | Existing device-team commitments and the cost of another consumer platform           |
| **Microsoft**                        | Industrial data, research and Azure relationships                          | Offtake, venture investment or a consortium could serve the need                     |
| **Skild, Mecka and other operators** | Task-specific collection and faster supplier access                        | Growth capital is not an unspent acquisition budget; retail obligations dilute focus |

These are strategic hypotheses. We found no evidence that any company listed above has bid for GoPro or expressed interest in acquiring it. The strategy evidence is public <sup>\[18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23\]</sup>.

A buyer could license existing content, commission a GoPro-based capture program, fund a partner, or negotiate selected IP and employees. Whole-company ownership becomes persuasive only if controlling firmware, recruitment, consent, collection design and repeat access produces advantages that contracts cannot secure economically. **That is the possible strategic premium, and it has to be demonstrated.**

## **9. The strongest case against us**

A buyer wants task-specific, action-grounded data. Most of GoPro’s business serves different customers. Its rights are nonexclusive, its cameras are substitutable, the sale process has already tested demand, and consumer support and cloud storage keep consuming resources. A targeted program could get the useful input with fewer inherited obligations.

These assets raise a second objection. GoPro has no exclusive access to these contributors; other operators can recruit them too. The library is licensed on a nonexclusive basis. The transferable design and firmware inventory is unverified, and NVIDIA’s current GEAR UMI design uses substitute cameras. If those four assets can each be had by contract, the premium for owning the company has to come from somewhere else.

Our appendix illustrates a \$35 million collection program yielding 250,000 accepted hours under assumed costs and a 25% acceptance rate. It would not reproduce GoPro’s brand, software or distribution. It does force the acquisition advocate to explain why those extra assets are worth hundreds of millions more.

We would advance a whole-company case only after a buyer-approved trial showed incremental model performance, a lawful and auditable rights chain, lower cost or materially faster delivery than the best feasible alternative, and a funded transaction that kept the necessary engineering team. Patent claims must map to the buyer’s actual roadmap. Counting patents is not enough.

## **10. Scorecard: how this thesis can be proven wrong**

Sorites preserves falsifiable claims, and it separates what the public record can show from what only diligence can show. Public developments are scored **observed** or **not observed** by the deadline shown. Not observed means no public evidence. It is never recorded as confirming or refuting the economic claim. Existing licensing revenue, UMI research and GR00T announcements are the baseline and do not count.

### **Public developments we will monitor**

| **\#** | **Development**                                                                                                                                                                | **Deadline** | **How to read it**                                                                                                                                    |
|--------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| M1     | An AI or robotics developer is named in a GoPro filing or release as having made a proposal for all or most of GoPro.                                                          | Feb 10, 2027 | Observed: shows buyer interest. Not observed: shows only that none is public. Private approaches and unnamed auction participants remain possible.    |
| M2     | GoPro or a named counterparty discloses a physical-AI or robotics data licensing, supply or partnership agreement with a stated customer or a value above \$5 million.         | Feb 10, 2027 | Observed: supports commercial demand. Whether it strengthens acquisition interest or shows a contract can capture the value is a diligence question.         |
| M3     | GoPro, its successor or a named partner announces a program recruiting owners of capture cameras, beyond opted-in subscribers, as paid contributors for task-specific footage. | Jun 30, 2027 | Observed: the audience is being used as a recruiting channel. Not observed: no announcement, which says nothing about whether the library is passive. |
| M4     | AI-content licensing revenue in the next filed quarter, against the \$2 million reported for Q2, and whether it is attributed to a single customer or delivery.                | Feb 10, 2027 | One quarter shows revenue, not durable scaling. Repeat customers and delivered hours would matter more.                                               |
| R      | Recorded, not predicted: whether the Starman merger closed, was further amended, was extended or was terminated.                                                               | Feb 10, 2027 | This is an observation checkpoint before the current Mar 31 outside date, not a closing forecast. A deal break raises urgency; it does not prove strategic value. |

### **Economic tests that need diligence**

These cannot be scored from public filings. A buyer would test them in a trial, and we will not describe them as confirmed without disclosed results.

> **E1.** Fully loaded cost per accepted episode from recruiting existing camera owners, compared with a newly commissioned kit-based program at equivalent scale, with matched tasks, sensing, rights, acceptance standards and delivery requirements.
>
> **E2.** Acceptance rate and repeat-contributor retention across at least two collection rounds.
>
> **E3.** A rights audit: whether existing permissions cover the proposed content and downstream use, and which newly commissioned tasks need additional agreements.
>
> **E4.** Time to first accepted delivery, against the best feasible alternative.

### **What would defeat the whole-company thesis**

> • A failed rights audit.
>
> • No meaningful improvement on held-out tasks in a buyer trial.
>
> • A superior, costed licensing or build alternative.
>
> • A financing crisis adds urgency but does not establish strategic value. Missing evidence leaves the thesis unproven.

The question worth taking to corporate development is precise: **what could a buyer do better by owning GoPro that it could not do, at lower cost, through a contract?** An affirmative answer could expose a neglected acquisition opportunity. Until then GoPro is a serious diligence candidate, and strategic relevance provides no floor under its common equity.
